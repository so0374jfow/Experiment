Granular synthesis deconstructs sound into tiny particles called 'grains' — each 20–80ms long. Your mouse position is mapped to a quaternion using spherical coordinates, then the quaternion's four components determine each grain's properties: w sets duration (20–80ms), x sets frequency (200–2000 Hz), y sets stereo pan (−1 to +1), and z sets amplitude envelope shape. Moving the mouse faster spawns more grains per second (up to 60/s). The 3D visualization places each grain at its quaternion's stereographic projection point — you're seeing the sound in quaternion space.
